# Weak spots — thinga, lesson 2026-08-21

From `1-thinga.txt`. Only the errors that would actually cost you something in a work
conversation or an interview — wrong meaning, wrong term, or wrong register.
Minor grammar slips are left out on purpose.

---

### 1. "work around" means the opposite of what you wanted
**You:** "We can work around some weak spots that I will get."
**Better:** "We can work on any weak spots that come up."
`work around → work on`

> *work around a problem* = avoid it, route past it. You told John you'd skip your weak
> spots, not fix them. This one genuinely inverts your meaning.

### 2. Going off script
**You:** "We also can go out of script easily, it's just a template anyway."
**Better:** "We can also go off script easily, it's just a template anyway."
`go out of script → go off script`

> John used the exact phrase back at you in the same lesson: *"you can go off script, as
> it's called."* One preposition apart.

### 3. Agreeing with a proposed plan
**You:** "A simple mock interview that we had before is the actual thing."
**Better:** "A simple mock interview like the one we had before is the way to go."
`is the actual thing → is the way to go`

> *the actual thing* isn't an English expression. **that's the way to go** is the natural
> way to endorse a plan in a meeting.

### 4. "behave" instead of "decide"
**You:** "We will behave based on our goals."
**Better:** "We'll decide based on our goals."
`we will behave → we'll decide`

> *behave* is about conduct, not choices. A colleague would hear this as "we'll be
> well-mannered according to our goals."

### 5. Career vocabulary
**You:** "We have a goal to get our first job in this area, in this major."
**Better:** "We have a goal to get our first job in this field."
`in this major → in this field`

> *major* is the subject you studied at university, never an industry. Use **field**,
> **area**, or **space** when talking about your career.

### 6. Asking for clarification — interview-critical
**You:** "I kind of missing the meaning of this question."
**Better:** "I'm not quite sure I follow the question."
`missing the meaning → not quite sure I follow`

> You will need this exact move in a real interview. *Missing the meaning* sounds like
> the question was badly written; **I'm not sure I follow** puts it on you politely and
> buys thinking time.

### 7. Thanking someone for rewording
**You:** "Thank you for providing me the question with a different paraphrase."
**Better:** "Thank you for rephrasing the question."
`providing me the question with a different paraphrase → rephrasing the question`

> Seven words doing the job of one verb. **rephrase** is the word you want.

### 8. Wrong technical verb
**You:** "It covers how we can allocate a variable."
**Better:** "It covers how we can declare a variable."
`allocate → declare`

> You **declare** a variable; the runtime **allocates** memory. Interviewers notice this
> distinction, and John was already probing you on memory allocation.

### 9. Missing passive when explaining
**You:** "This one uses for rendering much frequently than this one."
**Better:** "This one is used for rendering much more frequently."
`this one uses → this one is used` · `much frequently → much more frequently`

> Without the passive it reads as *the array is doing the using*. This pattern repeats
> throughout your explanation and is the single change that would most improve how
> technical your explanations sound.

### 10. verbally / verbosely
**You:** "If we will write it more verbally, it will look like this."
**Better:** "If we write it more verbosely, it will look like this."
`verbally → verbosely`

> False friend: *verbally* = out loud, spoken. **verbosely** = with more words. You also
> dropped the future after *if* here — *if we write*, not *if we will write*.

### 11. Real world
**You:** "In the actual world it will require us to grab some extra technologies."
**Better:** "In the real world it would require us to grab some extra technologies."
`in the actual world → in the real world`

> Fixed collocation, and a phrase you'll reach for constantly when contrasting a demo
> with production.

## Left out

Minor slips not worth your review time: article and plural agreement (*there is a lot of
cases*), `-s` on verbs, small-talk phrasing (*join the lesson*, *I focusing it*), and
places where Whisper misheard rather than you misspeaking (*a race* → arrays, *steppers*
→ slippers, *the death tools* → dev tools).

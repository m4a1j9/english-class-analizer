# Learner corrections: thinga, 2026-09-04

Nine mistakes from the learner's side of the lesson that are worth reviewing. They include a mock "tell me about yourself" answer and some STAR-method practice.

### 1. "Rise the plank" is a direct translation of the Russian *поднять планку*
**You:** "this time I want to once again rise up my plank and join a community where there will be people…"
**Better:** "this time I want to once again raise the bar and join a community where there will be people…"
`rise up my plank → raise the bar`

> In English a *plank* is only a wooden board. The listener hears nonsense or a joke. You used it twice in this lesson, including "you're always rising at the plank." The English idiom is *raise the bar*, and interviewers use it all the time.

### 2. "Crutch" for *костыль*
**You:** "so I could express how the things are working or why this crutch exists"
**Better:** "so I could explain how the things are working or why this workaround exists"
`express → explain` · `crutch → workaround`

> English developers say *workaround* or *hack*, not *crutch*. Also, *express* means putting your own feelings or ideas into words. When you make something clear to a new teammate, the word is *explain*.

### 3. "Thankfully" is not "thanks to"
**You:** "thankfully for that specific approach, I got some important communication skills"
**Better:** "thanks to that specific approach, I got some important communication skills"
`thankfully for → thanks to`

> *Thankfully* means "fortunately." The listener hears "luckily, for that approach…" and the cause-and-effect is lost. You did it again later with "thankfully of that important decisions." This is a key phrase in any STAR answer, so fix it.

### 4. "Raised from a middle position"
**You:** "I worked in two companies, and I raised from a middle position to a senior."
**Better:** "I worked in two companies, and I rose from a mid-level position to a senior one."
`raised → rose` · `middle → mid-level` · `a senior → a senior one`

> *Raise* needs an object: you raise something. The verb for your own career growth is *rise* (past tense *rose*). A "middle developer" is a Russian IT term. In English CVs and interviews the word is *mid-level*.

### 5. Citizen vs citizenship; refresh vs renew
**You:** "I still have a Russian citizen and I have to refresh this one"
**Better:** "I still have Russian citizenship and I have to renew this one"
`a Russian citizen → Russian citizenship` · `refresh → renew`

> *A citizen* is a person, so "I have a Russian citizen" sounds like there's someone with you. Documents such as passports and visas are *renewed*. You *refresh* a web page.

### 6. "The team of analysis"
**You:** "For example, we got two back-end teams, the team of analysis, the product managers, et cetera."
**Better:** "For example, we got two back-end teams, the team of analysts, the product managers, et cetera."
`analysis → analysts`

> *Analysis* is the activity and *analysts* are the people. In a list of teams, the listener expects a job title. You could also say *business analysts*.

### 7. "Align a meeting"
**You:** "it's better to align a meeting and prepare the prepare some document"
**Better:** "it's better to set up a meeting and prepare some document"
`align a meeting → set up a meeting`

> This is Russian IT jargon (*заалайнить встречу*). In English you *align* people or plans (you used "aligning our plans" correctly later), but you *set up* or *schedule* a meeting. You also said "we can align a short… call," and the fix there is the same.

### 8. "On hard communication"
**You:** "both of us was on hard communication with each other"
**Better:** "both of us were in close contact with each other"
`on hard communication → in close contact` · `was → were`

> "Hard communication" suggests that talking was difficult, which is the opposite of what you meant. *In close contact* says that you were talking often and easily.

### 9. "Notice" in place of "note" or "point out"
**You:** "I just want to notice that I'm still trying to answer some random generic questions"
**Better:** "I just want to note that I'm still trying to answer some random generic questions"
`notice → note`

> Russian *заметить* can mean both "to see" and "to remark." English *notice* only means "to see." To say something out loud, use *note*, *mention* or *point out*. You made the same mistake with "the person will just notice about all the major stuff," which should be "know about."
